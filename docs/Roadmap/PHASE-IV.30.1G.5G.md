# Phase IV.30.1G.5G — Portal Pairing & Boundary-Side Reasoning

## Pippin Pairs the Jambs

G.5F established that continuous playable floor through a short contour break is threshold evidence. G.5G makes that judgement explicitly geometric and two-sided: Pippin now asks whether the two unresolved boundary ends plausibly belong together, whether their approach tangents resemble paired jambs, and what kind of space exists across and on either side of the break.

A short break with compatible endpoint geometry and playable floor through it is classified as a **certified portal pair**. It remains open and is carried into the review draft as protected portal evidence. A very short, aligned break in a structural boundary may instead be classified as **certified boundary continuation** when its two sides still separate playable from non-playable space and the gap itself lacks convincing through-floor evidence. Ambiguous pairs remain unresolved; G.5G does not turn uncertainty into either a doorway or a wall.

This deliberately strengthens the semantic pipeline rather than adding another image reader. Living Contour performs the pairing after G.5F adjacency; Hybrid receives the resulting metadata through the same Living Contour candidates it already consumes.

### Acceptance contracts

- Open contour ends are paired only at portal-scale distances.
- Endpoint approach/tangent compatibility contributes to portal certification.
- Playable-floor evidence is sampled through the candidate gap.
- Both sides of the candidate break are sampled independently.
- Certified portal pairs are protected from automatic closure.
- Certified boundary continuation requires structural-wall semantics, a short gap and asymmetric boundary sides.
- Ambiguous breaks remain unresolved and are never promoted merely to complete a contour.
- Existing G.5D–G.5F safety and threshold contracts remain intact.
